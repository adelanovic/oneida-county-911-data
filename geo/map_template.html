About this data

What the numbers on this page are, and what they are not.

Source

Public daily 911 media reports (PDF) published by Oneida County, New York, at oneidacountyny.gov. Each row in a report is one agency dispatched to a call: time, agency, call type, street and town.

Coverage has gaps

Dispatches, not emergencies

When fire, an ambulance and police all go to one emergency, the report lists three rows, and this page counts three dispatches. Counts here measure workload by agency, not the number of distinct incidents.

Two report formats

Reports from 2024–2025 come from one dispatch system and give times to the second. The 2026 reports come from a newer system that gives times to the minute, groups rows by agency, uses some different call-type names (harmonized here), and leaves out proactive police activity such as traffic stops and premise checks, which were about a fifth of earlier dispatches. That is most of why 2026 shows fewer dispatches per day. Compare 2026 with earlier years with care.

Call types are what dispatch heard

A call type is the dispatcher's category when the call came in, not a confirmed outcome. Medical calls use Medical Priority Dispatch codes (for example 17B01 = Fall); this page groups them by complaint. The reports have no arrival or clear times, so response times cannot be measured.

How locations were placed

Mutual aid

A few dispatches went to towns in Herkimer, Oswego, Madison and Lewis counties. They count in the totals but are hidden from the map unless you turn them on or pick that county.
