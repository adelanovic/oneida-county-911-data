    Surge review

    Review recorded dispatch workload in 15-minute intervals, not unit availability, response performance or confirmed weather impacts. References are covered dates on the same weekday, in the same calendar year and within six weeks before or after the review date. The selected date and all annotated weather dates are excluded. At least four reference dates are required for a mean; fewer than eight are flagged as a small reference sample. Matching reference dates with zero selected dispatches remain in the denominator. Missing reports are never treated as zero. References may contain other unusual events and are not a causal estimate or statistical significance test. Place, responder and call-type filters apply equally to both sides. The review uses its own date and full-day timeline, replacing page year, exact date, month, weekday and hour filters. Reported town assignments are used without estimated street coordinates. Matching unmapped dispatches and destinations hidden from the map remain included. Call-type change is observed count minus reference mean, not dispatches proven caused by an event. Counts are agency dispatches; multiple agencies may attend one emergency. No durations or simultaneous busy-unit counts can be inferred. Explore and Replay replace the page's time filters with the review date; Replay starts paused at the selected interval.

    Dispatches per 1,000 residents

    Observed dispatches per 1,000 residents across the covered dates, using Census 2024 population estimates. These count agency responses, not unique emergencies or residents needing help. Time, call-type and agency filters apply; the place filter is ignored here to preserve comparisons. The dark tick marks the county benchmark. Click a place to filter the rest of the page.

    Rates describe reported response areas, not residents' personal risk. Visitors and repeat or multi-agency responses contribute to the counts. Village rates count only dispatches explicitly identified with that village; town-only labels may conceal additional village dispatches. Villages are included in town totals and must not be added to them.

    Some populations are adjusted to match reporting labels rather than official town boundaries: Waterville is grouped entirely with Sangerfield, Remsen village entirely with Remsen, and Sherrill separately from Vernon. This changes the populations shown for Marshall, Sangerfield, Remsen, Trenton, Vernon and Sherrill. New York Mills retains separate village parts in New Hartford and Whitestown. These are reporting-area comparisons, not verified rates within legal town boundaries.

    About this data

    What the numbers on this page are, and what they are not.

    Source

    Public daily 911 media reports (PDF) published by Oneida County, New York, at oneidacountyny.gov. Each row in a report is one agency dispatched to a call: time, agency, call type, street and town.

    Coverage has gaps

    Dispatches, not emergencies

    When fire, an ambulance and police all go to one emergency, the report lists three rows, and this page counts three dispatches. Counts here measure workload by agency, not the number of distinct incidents.

    Two report formats

    The older dispatch system covers January 2024 through the morning of July 15, 2025 and gives times to the second. Reports from the newer system start that day, give times to the minute, group rows by agency, and use some different call-type names (harmonized here). They omit proactive police activity such as traffic stops and premise checks. The 2025 series crosses this reporting change: complete calendar coverage does not make the two periods directly comparable. Monthly PDFs are assigned to each dispatch's recorded date.

    Call types are what dispatch heard

    A call type is the dispatcher's category when the call came in, not a confirmed outcome. Medical dispatches use Medical Priority Dispatch codes (for example 17B01 = Fall); this page groups them by complaint. The reports have no arrival or clear times, so response times cannot be measured.

    How locations were placed

    Mutual aid

    A few dispatches went to towns in Herkimer, Oswego, Madison and Lewis counties. They count in the totals but are hidden from the map unless you turn them on or pick that county.

    Built with

    Street and town boundaries: US Census TIGER/Line 2024. Selected Utica, Whitesboro, Marcy and Rome location gaps: Oneida County public CAD road reference (saved snapshot). Population: US Census Bureau 2024 estimates for cities, towns and villages. Map: Leaflet. All processing is reproducible from the original PDFs with the scripts in this project's repository.
